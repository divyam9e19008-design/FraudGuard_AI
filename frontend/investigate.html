KNOWN LIMITATION · OPEN ITEM

These factors come from global importance, not per-case attribution.

The factor list above is built by taking the model’s top-10 features overall and keeping the ones that are active on this application. That means the candidate set is identical for every case — only the filtering changes. It is a reasonable first pass, and it is not true per-case explainability.

This matters because 05_escalation_procedure.txt §3.1 requires a reviewer to examine the factors surfaced for the specific application, explicitly “not just the overall model-wide feature importance.” Measured against its own policy library, the current pipeline does not yet satisfy that clause.

The documented fix is SHAP — per-application attribution giving each feature a signed contribution for this case, which also replaces the hand-written direction labels with computed ones. Tracked as the next improvement rather than presented as solved.

ENGINEERING INTEGRITY

The gauge never reaches red — and that’s the finding.

Across the demo sample the highest fraud probability the model produces is 0.20. On a raw 0–100 scale every case therefore lands in the LOW band, and HIGH is unreachable. That is not a broken gauge — it is what probability compression under a 1.1% base rate looks like. A forest trained on rebalanced data still votes “not fraud” in the large majority of its trees.

The consequence is that fixed bands are the wrong decision rule here. What matters is the operating threshold and the relative ranking — a case at 0.20 sits at the very top of the population even though it reads as “low”. This is the trade-off recorded in 05_escalation_procedure.txt §4 and the ranking-versus-precision limitation in 06_model_governance_policy.txt §2.4.

Case TXN-665979 is kept in this demo deliberately: it is the one genuine fraud in the sample, and the model scored it 9/100. A missed case shown on the page is worth more than a curated one hidden from it.
