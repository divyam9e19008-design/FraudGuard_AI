Document: Identity Verification and Address History Policy
Department: Fraud Prevention & Risk
Applies to: New account-opening applications

1. Purpose
This policy sets out how address history should be treated when assessing the fraud risk of a new account application.

2. Background
Internal analysis of historical account-opening data found that applicants with no prior recorded address history show a fraud rate approximately 4.5 times higher than applicants with an established address history. This association was confirmed as statistically significant (chi-square test, p < 0.001) and is not attributable to chance.

3. Policy
3.1 An application where the applicant's previous-address-months field is absent or encoded as unknown (no prior address on record) must be treated as an identity-verification risk signal, not simply as missing data.
3.2 Absence of address history alone is not sufficient grounds for automatic decline. It should be combined with other signals (income bracket, device signals, contact verification) before a decision is made.
3.3 Applications with no address history AND at least one additional elevated risk signal (see Device & Session Risk Policy, Contact Verification Policy) should be routed to manual review rather than auto-approved.
3.4 Applicants with no address history who are otherwise low-risk on all other signals may proceed through standard processing, since new-to-country or first-time applicants legitimately lack address history.

4. Rationale
Fraudulent applications frequently use newly created or synthetic identities, which by construction lack a prior address trail. Genuine first-time applicants (e.g., young adults, new immigrants) also lack address history, so this signal must always be combined with corroborating evidence rather than used in isolation.

5. Review cadence
This policy should be re-validated quarterly against updated fraud-rate statistics.
